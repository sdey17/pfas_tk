Figure S14. Log KL (derived from Le, et al. 10 ) vs Log Kas_app under different salt concentration for
PFHxA, PFHpA, PFOA, and PFNA and PFHxS. The size of symbols increases with salt
concentration increase from 32, to 112 mM (NaCl).



       The parameter KL in Le, et al. 10 study account for air-water adsorption of PFAS in the

absence of additional salt, which is an indicator of hydrophobicity of PFAS. Fig. S8 shows a linear

relation between Log KL and Log K as_app under all salt conditions indicating that the hydrophobic

interaction (which increased with chain length) is the dominant driven force for PFAS/BSA

interaction. Interestingly, PFHxS falls into the same fitting lines with PFCAs. Given the KL values

are based on chemical structure, which is easy to calculate/obtain. More work is required to

determine the relation between Log KL and Log Kas_app to explore if K as_app can be predicted based

on the KL values.

Application of binding model

         Fluorescence profiles of PFAS/BSA were measured at range of ionic strength with different

salt species to quantify the binding constants (K a_Hill) based on the Hill equation. Model parameters

(i.e., Kas1 , K as2, KA1, and K A2 ) wewre fitted assuming that Kas_app is equivalent to K a_Hill. To do this

the sum of the squared difference K a_Hill and K as_app was minimized. Parameter fits for an individ ua l

salt condition are likely not unique. As such, model parameters are determined by simultaneo us ly

fitting multiple salt conditions, including monovalent and divalent salt conditions. Parameters

uncertainties were derived from the covariance matrix of the fit, assuming normally distributed

residuals. Notably, since the K as_app value is calculated from the fitted parameters rather than being

directly fitted, its associated uncertainty is not presented. Once the model parameters (i.e., Kas1 , Kas2,

K A1, and K A2 ) for a given PFAS are determined, they can be used for prediction of binding affinities

at a range of salt conditions.

salt conditions.
