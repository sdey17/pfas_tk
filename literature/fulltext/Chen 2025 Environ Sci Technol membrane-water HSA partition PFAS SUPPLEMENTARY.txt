Experimental design for membrane-water partitioning

    The phospholipid membrane-water partition coefficients (KMW) for PFAS used in this

study was measured using the TRANSIL Membrane Affinity Kit (Sovicell GmBH, Leipzig,

Germany). The solid supported lipid membrane (SSLM) assay immobilizes individual

membrane bilayers onto porous silica beads, enabling one side of the phospholipid membrane

to face the water phase. The test kits were received frozen and immediately stored at -20 ℃




                                                                                                  Downloaded from pubs.​acs.​org/​esthag/​article-supplement/​3661081/​pdf/​es4c06734_si_001/​ by CALEPA AIR RESOURCES BOARD user on 22 October 2026
until use. Each plate of the kits used in this work contains 500 µL vials distributed in 96-

position racks arranged in an 8 × 12 grid, that were comprised of 12 groups, with each group

consisting of 8 test tubes. The 8 tubes in each group include 2 blanks and 6 tubes with

phospholipid membranes bound to beads at varying volumes of phospholipid membranes

(0.067, 0.133, 0.266, 0.529, 1.058, and 2.166 μL), all filled with 500 μL of phosphate-buffered

saline (PBS) buffer solution.

    Membrane-water partition coefficients (KMW) were measured according to a recently

developed experimental procedure.1 One or more rows of test tubes were thawed at room

temperature for 20 min in a ventilation hood before the experiment. Because the PBS buffer

solution and phospholipid membrane beads settled at the bottom of the test tube, the tubes were

gently shaken to suspend the beads. The suspended liquid was then transferred to 1.5 mL

polypropylene centrifuge tubes, and the original vials were rinsed with 500 μL 10mM

ammonium acetate, combining the two buffers. After gently shaking, low-speed centrifugation

was performed at 500 ×g for 5 min to precipitate the phospholipid membrane beads. Then, 800

μL of the supernatant was discarded and replaced with 800 μL of 10mM ammonium acetate

and centrifuged at 500 ×g for 5 mins. Centrifuging and replacing with the new buffer was

repeated twice; the new buffer system contains approximately 0.8 % of the initial PBS content.

Names, acronyms, Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) number of the PFAS and group names

used in the experiment are listed in Table S1. For membrane-water partitioning experiments,

60 PFAS were divided into groups (Table S1) by structures to reduce the extent of

experimentation and minimize the consumption of experimental materials. We evaluated the

experimental results for single compounds (PFOA, PFOS, and 6:2 FTS) and their mixtures.

Equilibrium dialysis on Rapid Equilibrium Dialysis (RED) system

    The RED system was tested as a potentially simpler and higher-throughput method to

evaluate HSA-PFAS interactions. The incubation method was based on the research of Gao et

al.2 In brief, the HSA solution was diluted with a buffer solution (0.1 M ammonium acetate,

pH=7.4) to 1 μmol/L, and PFAS standard solutions were diluted with the same buffer to a series

of 400 μL solutions at 0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, and 2 μmol/L. HSA and PFAS standard

solutions were prepared on either side of the RED analysis devices. Tests were performed in

triplicate. First, 200 μL of HSA was added to the RED HSA tube chamber, and 400 μL of PFAS

solution were added to the buffer chamber to achieve equal heights. Second, the equilibrium

dialysis device was moved to a shaker at room temperature with a speed of 250 rpm for 4 h to

achieve equilibrium. Third, 50 μL of each post-dialysis sample from the buffer chamber was

transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, 50 μL of methanol was added, the sample was vortexed

to mix, and then transferred to a vial for PFAS analysis.

    We were unable to obtain specific binding curves for all PFAS with HSA using this system,

possibly due to high non-specific adsorption of the more surface-active long-chain PFAS to

assay materials, similar in recent work.3 We therefore focused our equilibrium dialysis work

on Slide-A-Lyzer devices (described in the next section), which provide a relatively larger

volume and smaller surface area-to-volume ratio compared to RED.

with ammonium acetate buffer only in the bottom vial. The equilibrium dialysis test series

was prepared using a constant 1.0 μM HSA in upper vessels and a series of concentrations of

PFAS in the bottom vials. For PFAS controls, the series concentration of PFAS solutions

were added to the bottom vials and only blank buffer solution to the upper dialysis vessel.

The pH of the buffer solution was adjusted with ammonia or acetic acid. The pH values tested

were 6.0 and 7.4. Triplicates for each pH were executed.

HPLC-MSMS analytical method

    The delay column was a Thermo Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA) Hypersil GOLD C18 ,

4.6 × 50 mm, 2.6 μm, and the analytical column was a Thermo Scientific Accucore RP-MS,

2.6 μm, 2.1 × 100 mm, column box temperature: 45 °C, mobile phase A: DI water containing

20 mM ammonium acetate, mobile Phase B: methanol. Sample injection volume: 10 μL. LC

gradient was as follows, 0.5 mL/min at 5% B for 0.5 min, then 40% B for 2.0 min at initial,

increased B to 95% at 12 min, raised flow speed to 0.45mL/min at 15min, then switched back

to initial conditions at 16 min.

    The mass spectrometry ion source uses electrospray ionization (ESI). The ion source

spray needle temperature was 325 ℃, the ion source temperature was 300 ℃, and the sheath

gas, aux gas, and sweep gas were set to 50, 10, and 1 unit flow speed (Arb), respectively.

MSMS transitions are provided in Table S3.

Method validation, quality control and quality assurance

    A matrix-matched calibration curve—featuring a water/methanol mixture matrix with

standard PFAS concentrations of 0.02, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 2, 10, 50, and 100 ng/mL in triplicate—

was crafted to assess the method's linearity. The method's lowest quantification (LOQs)

estimated from a range of low-concentration standard solutions—spanning from 0.02 to 0.2




                                                                                                  Downloaded from pubs.​acs.​org/​esthag/​article-supplement/​3661081/​pdf/​es4c06734_si_001/​ by CALEPA AIR RESOURCES BOARD user on 22 October 2026
ng/mL—spiked into the solvent. The limits of detection (LODs) were the concentrations

where the signal-to-noise ratio equaled 3:1.

    The calibration curves exhibited linearity (R2 > 0.990). The LOQs spanned a range from

0.2 to 2 ng/mL. Three whole method blank samples were prepared alongside each batch and

were concurrently arranged within the sequence, revealing no contamination. Check

standards (CCVs) were placed at the initiation and end of each batch sequence, with an

additional CCV inserted after every 20 samples. The accuracy from CCVs fluctuated between

80.9% and 121.9 %, and the RSDs lower than 20%



Molecular docking for HSA

    To verify the best binding pose of PFAS with HSA at various pH values, blind docking

procedures were performed using Autodock Vina4 on a Linux x86_64 operation system, using

Autodock Vina v1.2.6. The crystal structure of HSA (Protein Data Bank ID: 1AO65) was

selected as it is an unliganded HSA which may be similar to the purified protein purchased

for equilibrium dialysis. The ligand structure of 63 PFAS were screened using the CompTox

Chemistry dashboard6 and then prepared using the python Meeko package7. A nine-grid box,

each measuring 26 × 26 × 26 Å with a spacing of 1.0 Å, was employed to encompass the

entire HSA structure for a blind docking procedure. This ensured that simulations covered the

main drug-binding sites of HSA and screened all other potential sites for all the PFAS in this

work (Table S4). After that, the Lamarckian genetic algorithm (LGA) was applied to seek the

best binding site for PFAS in HSA with the default parameters. For each docking procedure,

20 conformations were output, of which the conformation with the lowest binding energy was

selected and analyzed using the Autodock Vina (Figure S3). The HSA site names including

fatty acids (FA) binding sites and drug binding sites8 are demonstrated in Figure S4.
