WHY STRATIFY: THE TUBULAR FACTOR IS NOT ONE POPULATION
==============================================================================
  all 9 (f_u mixed)        n=9  T 0.00675 to 1.22287  = 181.2-fold
  Smeltz coverage, 8       n=8  T 0.00675 to 0.56809  =  84.2-fold
  long chain, nC>=8        n=5  T 0.00675 to 1.22287  = 181.2-fold
  long chain excl PFDoDA   n=4  T 0.00675 to 0.01352  =   2.0-fold
  short/mid, nC<=7         n=4  T 0.02291 to 0.56809  =  24.8-fold

  A model that fits ONE constant T can only work where T is
  actually near-constant. The TIGHTEST stratum is "long chain excl PFDoDA"
  at 2.0-fold; the widest is all 9 (f_u mixed) at 181-fold.

  ONE COMPOUND DRIVES MOST OF THE SPREAD. PFDoDA's T is 1.223,
  2.2x above the next highest (PFHpA) and 181x above the lowest. Stage 29
  established that it is an UPPER BOUND on an undetectable urinary
  clearance -- not a measurement. Dropping it takes the nC>=8
  stratum from 181-fold to 2.0-fold. That is why it has its own
  stratum, and the reason is Stage 29's verdict, not the fit.

==============================================================================
WHAT TO TAKE FROM THIS
==============================================================================
  1. THE RANKING REVERSES BETWEEN THE TWO REGIMES, AND THAT IS
     THE RESULT WORTH REPORTING.
     stratum                   best model                           median    worst
     long chain excl PFDoDA    QSAR log t½ ~ nC                       1.5x     1.7x
     short/mid, nC<=7          M1b: T = fitted constant               8.2x     8.8x

     Each winner carried into the OTHER stratum:
     QSAR log t½ ~ nC                   on short/mid, nC<=7            23.5x   114.1x
     M1b: T = fitted constant           on long chain excl PFDoDA       2.2x     2.4x

     So these are not competing models with one winner. The QSAR
     works where half-life is a smooth function of chain length
     (2011 -> 1305 -> 862 -> 295 d across PFOA to PFDoDA); the
     mechanistic model works where it is not, but T is near-
     constant. PFBA 4.2 d, PFBS 50.6 d, PFHxS 1635 d, PFHpA 152 d
     is no function of chain length at all, and the QSAR fails
     there (23.5x median) while a fitted T does not.

  2. THE CORRECTIONS MADE THE POOLED NUMBERS WORSE, NOT BETTER.
     M1b on all 9 goes 2.1x (Stage 28) -> 3.8x here; M1a 26.9x -> 43.6x.
     This is not a reason to revert. Stage 28's lower numbers came
     from a T that credited the kidney with faecal elimination and
     from Ryu f_u, whose T happens to be more uniform across
     compounds. A model is not improved by an input that is wrong
     in a convenient direction. The corrected inputs are right and
     the error they expose is real.

  3. SMELTZ'S ADVANTAGE IS CONFINED TO THE LONG CHAINS. Its PFHpA
     f_u of 0.0004 -- the lowest any study reports, 11-fold below
     Ryu's -- implies T = 0.57, i.e. almost no net
     reabsorption of a compound with a 152-day half-life. Either
     that f_u is too low or PFHpA is cleared by a route this model
     does not contain. It is the single largest contributor to the
     short-chain failure and should be flagged in the paper.

  4. EVERY NUMBER IN THE TWO STRATA RESTS ON FOUR COMPOUNDS.
     4 of the stratified fits have n - k <= 2, i.e. at most two
     residual degrees of freedom under leave-one-out. The reversal
     in point 1 is a real feature of these data, but its SIZE is
     not estimable from four points, and the paper should say so
     rather than quote 1.5x as an accuracy.

  5. AND THE WHOLE HUMAN ARM IS n=1. Every half-life and every V_d
     is Abraham's single volunteer. No fold error here is an
     estimate of population accuracy.
